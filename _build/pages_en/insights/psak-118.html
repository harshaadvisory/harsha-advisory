Introduction

There is an important change in how companies that apply SAK Umum (the full Indonesian Financial Accounting Standards) present their financial statements, yet many businesses are still unaware of its impact. It is called PSAK 118 (PSAK being the Indonesian Financial Accounting Standards): Penyajian dan Pengungkapan dalam Laporan Keuangan (Presentation and Disclosure in Financial Statements).

The standard was approved by the Indonesian Institute of Accountants' Financial Accounting Standards Board (DSAK IAI) on 28 May 2025 and will take effect on 1 January 2027. It replaces PSAK 201 (previously known as PSAK 1) and is an adoption of the international standard IFRS 18.

What makes PSAK 118 important is not simply a change in the standard's number. It fundamentally changes the structure of the income statement, introduces new required subtotals and categories, and calls for a different approach to disclosure. For companies that prepare financial statements under SAK Umum, this is not a cosmetic change, but an adjustment that needs preparation.

This article explains what PSAK 118 is, what is changing, who is affected, and, most importantly, what companies need to prepare before 2027. From our experience helping companies through reporting changes and financial restructuring, adjustments like this go most smoothly for those who prepare early, not those who wait for the deadline.

Note: this article is educational and summarizes the general provisions of PSAK 118 from official IAI sources. It is not a substitute for a technical review of the full standard. For application to your company's specific circumstances, please refer to the official text of the standard and consult your accountant or auditor.

What PSAK 118 is and why it exists

To understand PSAK 118, it helps to understand its background.

PSAK 118 is the adoption of IFRS 18, an international standard that came out of a long International Accounting Standards Board (IASB) project called Primary Financial Statements, which ran from 2014 to 2024. Its overarching goal is simple: to make financial statements easier to compare across companies, and to communicate performance more clearly to investors and other users of the statements.

The problem it aims to solve: until now, the old presentation standard (PSAK 201) allowed considerable latitude in preparing the income statement. As a result, two companies in the same industry could present their income statements with different structures, making direct comparison difficult. Investors struggled to compare like with like.

PSAK 118 is designed to standardize that structure. By requiring certain subtotals and categories, income statements become more consistent and easier to compare across companies. This is the core of the change.

What is changing: three main areas

The biggest changes under PSAK 118 are in the income statement and the notes to the financial statements. Changes to the statement of cash flows are more limited, and changes to the statement of financial position and the statement of changes in equity are relatively minor. Let us focus on three main areas.

First, subtotals that must now be presented. PSAK 118 requires the income statement to show certain subtotals consistently, including operating profit or loss, profit or loss before financing and income tax, and profit or loss for the period. Previously, the presentation of subtotals such as "operating profit" was not always uniform across companies. Now the structure is standardized, so readers can find equivalent figures in the same position.

Second, classification of income and expenses into categories. PSAK 118 requires all income and expenses to be grouped into categories: operating, investing, and financing, plus separate requirements for income tax and discontinued operations. Broadly speaking, the operating category covers core business activities, the investing category relates to assets that generate returns largely independently, and the financing category relates to raising funds. The aim is to clearly separate what comes from running the business, what comes from investing, and what comes from financing the business. Note that this classification follows the requirements and exceptions set out in the standard, so it cannot be determined solely from the general type of transaction. For specific cases, the reference remains the official text of PSAK 118.

Third, disclosure of Ukuran Kinerja Tetapan Manajemen (UKTM, management-defined performance measures). This is a relatively new concept. PSAK 118 governs the disclosure of performance measures defined by management, meaning the performance measures management uses to communicate its view of the company's performance, which may differ from the standard measures in the standard. UKTM does not mean management is free to present any figure without limits. Quite the opposite: the standard requires disclosures that allow users of the statements to understand these measures and reconcile them with the figures specified in PSAK 118. That way, when management presents its own version of a performance measure, readers can understand where the figure comes from and how it relates to the official figures.

Beyond these three areas, PSAK 118 also allows flexibility in presenting operating expenses, whether by function, by nature, or a combination of both, depending on what is most relevant for the company.

Who is affected

This question comes up often, and it is important to answer it precisely to avoid misunderstanding.

PSAK 118 applies to entities that prepare financial statements under SAK Umum (full PSAK). This generally includes companies with public accountability, such as listed companies, banks, and financial institutions, as well as other companies that apply SAK Umum.

Among companies that apply SAK Umum, the impact of PSAK 118 cuts across industries. Manufacturing, banking, trading, property, and services companies can all be affected, according to the framework they use. The official IAI announcement confirms that the adoption of PSAK 118 affects the presentation of companies' financial statements across all industries.

To clarify: "all industries" here means across sectors within the SAK Umum framework, not every company without exception. Entities that use a different framework, such as SAK Entitas Privat (SAK EP, the Financial Accounting Standards for Private Entities) intended for entities without public accountability, have their own separate standards. So not every company automatically applies PSAK 118 directly.

Even so, entities using other frameworks are still advised to understand the direction of this change, because financial reporting is moving toward greater comparability and transparency.

Why this matters to business owners, not just accountants

At first glance, PSAK 118 sounds like a technical matter that can simply be left to the accounting team or auditor. But there are several reasons why business owners and leaders should understand it too.

Your income statement will look different. If your company applies SAK Umum, the structure of the income statement you usually read will change from 2027. The same figures will be presented in a different arrangement. Understanding this change matters so that you can keep reading your business performance smoothly, without being confused by the new format.

Comparisons across periods and across companies will change. Because the structure is standardized, comparing statements from 2027 onward with prior years, or comparing your company with competitors, will follow the new framework. This affects how you assess trends and your competitive position.

Management decisions are involved. Some aspects of PSAK 118, such as the choice of expense presentation and the disclosure of management's own performance measures, involve judgments that are not purely technical accounting matters, but rather how the company wants to communicate its performance. This is an area that deserves leadership attention.

External parties will see a different report format. For companies that apply PSAK 118, changes in structure and disclosure can affect how your financial statements are read and compared by banks, investors, and prospective partners. Companies whose statements are well organized and in line with the new standard will be easier to assess.

What to prepare before 2027

Although 2027 may sound far away, preparation should start now, because adjusting to a new standard touches systems, processes, and habits that cannot be changed overnight. Here are some practical steps.

Start by understanding whether your company is affected. The first step: confirm which standards framework your company uses. If you apply SAK Umum, PSAK 118 is directly relevant. If you are unsure, this is the right question to put to your accountant or auditor early on.

Learn the new income statement format. Start getting familiar with the new structure: the required subtotals and the grouping into operating, investing, and financing categories. The earlier your finance team understands it, the smoother the transition.

Review your recording system. Classifying income and expenses into the new categories may require changes to how transactions are recorded and grouped from the start. Your accounting system and chart of accounts may need to be reviewed so they are ready to produce reports in the new format.

Prepare comparative data and assess the impact. Reports for the period of initial application generally need to present comparative information in line with the standard's transition requirements. It is therefore worth starting early to review the readiness of historical data, reporting systems, and any presentation adjustments needed. Details on comparative periods and restatement methods follow the transition requirements of PSAK 118, so they should be based on the official text of the standard and confirmed with your accountant or auditor. What is clear is that this work is lighter when done in stages than all at once close to the deadline.

Talk to your auditor or consultant early. A change in standards is the right moment to open a dialogue with whoever prepares or audits your statements. A question asked now costs far less than a correction found during the audit.

Review how performance is communicated. The UKTM requirements govern the disclosure of management-defined performance measures. Companies need to review the performance measures they have been communicating to users of their statements, and make sure the disclosures follow the standard's requirements. This is an opportunity to organize how the company communicates its performance transparently, within a framework that is now more structured, not room to present figures without limits.

Closing

PSAK 118 is not just a change in an accounting standard's number. It changes how financial statements are prepared and read, with a sound purpose: statements that are more consistent, easier to compare, and more transparent in communicating performance.

For companies that apply SAK Umum, this is a change to prepare for, not to wait for. The time until 1 January 2027 may feel ample, but adjustments that touch recording systems, report formats, and the habits of the finance team are best done in stages, well before the deadline. The form of preparation needed will, of course, depend on each company's circumstances and needs.

As with many aspects of managing business finances, readiness for a change in standards is not built overnight. It is prepared early, while the pressure is still manageable, not when the deadline is right in front of you.

A healthy business is not just one that makes a profit, but one that understands its numbers, controls its risks, and moves in a clear direction.
