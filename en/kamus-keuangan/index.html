Business finance glossary

Finance terms, in plain language

Short explanations of terms that often come up in financial statements, discussions with banks, and our training sessions. Prepared by the Harsha Advisory team for business owners and management.

Cash flow (arus kas)
The movement of cash into and out of a business over a given period. A business can record a profit and still run short of cash, for example when it sells on credit while its suppliers must be paid in cash.
Operating cash flow (arus kas operasional)
Cash generated by the core business, excluding loans and investments. A key indicator of whether a business can fund itself.
Asset register
A list of all of a company's fixed assets with their acquisition date, value, and depreciation schedule. Without an asset register, profit often looks larger than it really is because depreciation goes unrecorded.
Break even point (BEP)
The point at which total revenue equals total costs, so the business makes neither a profit nor a loss. Calculated as fixed costs divided by the contribution margin per unit.
Cash conversion cycle (CCC)
The number of days from when cash goes out to buy materials or stock until it comes back in from customers. Calculated as inventory days plus receivable days minus payable days. The shorter it is, the less working capital is tied up.
Fractional CFO
Financial advisory support that helps management read the company's financial position and assess important decisions with more focus, without hiring a full-time CFO. Learn more
Financial distress
A situation in which a company struggles to meet its financial obligations. It is usually preceded by warning signs such as repeated negative operating cash flow and reliance on short-term loans.
Financial wellness
The financial health of employees: being able to manage spending, having an emergency fund, and planning for the future. Employees with low financial wellness tend to be less productive and more likely to change jobs. Learn more
Adjusting journal entries (jurnal penyesuaian)
Entries made at the end of a period to bring account balances in line with actual conditions, for example depreciation, prepaid expenses, and accrued revenue.
Management report (laporan manajemen)
An internal report for decision-making, containing key indicators, trend analysis, and comparisons against targets. It differs from formal financial statements, which are prepared for external parties. Learn more
Net profit margin (margin laba bersih)
Net profit as a percentage of revenue. Rising sales do not guarantee a rising margin; when costs grow faster than sales, the business looks busy but its profit thins out.
Working capital (modal kerja)
Current assets minus current liabilities, meaning the funds available to run day-to-day operations. Rapid sales growth often ends up absorbing working capital.
Internal control (pengendalian internal)
Policies and procedures that safeguard assets, ensure accurate records, and prevent fraud, for example segregation of duties, tiered authorization, and routine reconciliation. Learn more
Cash flow projection (proyeksi arus kas)
An estimate of cash coming in and going out over the next few weeks or months, so that cash shortfalls are spotted before they happen. We generally prepare 90-day projections. Learn more
Retirement planning
Financial planning for retirement: calculating the funds needed, sources of retirement income, protection, and long-term investment strategy. Learn more
Green SBN (SBN hijau)
Indonesian government securities (SBN) whose proceeds are allocated to environmentally friendly projects, including green sukuk. For BPRs (rural banks) and corporate treasury, they can serve as a diversification instrument. Learn more
Sustainable finance
A financial approach that considers environmental, social, and governance (ESG) factors in investment and financing decisions. In Indonesia it is regulated by, among others, POJK 51/2017 (an OJK regulation). Learn more
